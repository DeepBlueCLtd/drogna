The Operator Flow Chart

The Operator tab is a table. The SRD has said since Version 1 that the organising diagram is a flow chart with a loop in it — and a table has no cycle in it. This is that picture, drawn live: every component wearing an instrument designed for what it does, and a new one at the head of the observation path that can be told where to go.

What the table cannot say

Stop the sensors in today’s Operator tab and one word in one row changes. What it means — ingestion goes quiet, the observation store stops growing, the monitor has nothing to score and says so, the loop stops turning — is spread over two other tabs and reassembled by a reader who already knows the architecture. The flow chart’s whole claim is that consequence should be visible where the cause was applied.

Three rules hold the picture honest, and they are the reason this is a design document rather than a screenshot of a graph library.

observations — points, measured fields — forecasts, holdings ownship — the platform, its track, its demand control — requests, decisions, commands refusal — denied, declined, protected

Rule one: three kinds of figure, never mixed

The table has one kind of figure — whatever the component said about itself. The flow chart puts broker throughput (which only the shell can count) beside a residual (which only the monitor can compute) beside a breach threshold (which is configuration). Mixing them is how a display starts asserting things nothing published, which is the one thing Constitution VII exists to prevent. So each kind gets a treatment, and the legend names all three on screen.

A figure may not change kind between states. When a component goes silent its reported figures do not quietly become the shell’s last-known values dressed as current: they keep the simulation time they were reported at, and the face says how far behind that now is. The panel test asserts the kind of every figure, so a figure cannot be reclassified by accident (FR-008, SC-006).

Rule two: every node is the same frame around a different instrument

The chrome is identical everywhere, so a reader learns it once: state word, the simulation time the figures were last updated at, how far behind that is now, and the control the rules allow. What sits between them is chosen for what the component actually does — a store shows volume and growth, the monitor shows drift against the threshold that will fire, the runner shows its ensemble. A single generic gauge repeated twenty times would have told a reader nothing the table did not already.

Six states, and none of them is a colour alone

Silent and unheard are different words, because they are different facts. Today’s System tab already draws that distinction and its footnote says the display cannot tell a component that has not run yet from one that has stopped — only the silence. The flow chart keeps that honesty and adds the one thing it can honestly add: when the shell dispatched the stop itself, it says so, and still waits for the silence to confirm it (FR-006, FR-014).

Rule three: the arrows are not drawn, they are derived

A picture of the wiring that is maintained separately from the wiring goes stale, and nothing tells you when. So a topic edge exists wherever one role’s publish rules and another’s subscribe rules overlap in contracts/topology.json — the master a gate already keeps honest against the run configuration. A port edge is drawn dashed for the couplings that carry no broker traffic at all: the world-sampler port, and the store interfaces. Without those, the environment generator — which publishes nothing but heartbeats — would sit isolated in a picture of a system it is the source of. A wildcard subscription is drawn as a tap on a namespace bus; every edge is still there and still counted by the gate (FR-003 to FR-005).

Twenty faces

In arc order: the plane first, then the observation path, then the loop, then deciding, advising and leaving. Each is drawn at the expanded density, with the kind of every figure declared beneath it. Where a face says something in words, those are the component’s own words carried in its message — not a sentence the panel composed.

The platform, at full size

The one node that takes a command other than stop and start. Everything on the left is what the motion simulator currently is; everything hollow is what it has been asked to be. The gap between them is the interesting part, and the face refuses to let it be silent: the binding limit is named, and an unreachable demand states its shortfall in the platform’s own report rather than being quietly clipped to something achievable (FR-020, FR-021).

The inlet, and the component that is not there. The demand arrives on ctl/platform/demand. The broker’s rules admit the operator surface today and are written to admit an adaptive sampler as a second publisher. On the canvas that is drawn as an open bracket in the empty slot above the platform — deliberately not a node: no status dot, no chrome, no headline. A greyed-out node would read exactly like a component that has stopped, and the System tab’s own footnote says the display cannot tell those two apart. Making that footnote false to gain a box would be the precise failure Constitution VII exists to prevent (FR-022).
And the arrow that is missing. A reader who knows the architecture will look for planner → platform and not find it. Its absence has to be an answer rather than an oversight, so the planner’s edge is drawn terminating at ctl/plan against a red stop, captioned the planner recommends; it does not command the platform. Turning a recommendation into a demand is a decision, and the component that would make it does not exist yet (FR-023, Constitution VIII).

Stop the platform, and watch what it costs

Every fact in these four frames is already available today — in three different tabs, to a reader who knows where to look. The argument for the flow chart is that consequence should be visible where the cause was applied. This is SC-001, and it is captured as a watched turn rather than inferred from green tests.

And then start it. The same chain runs backwards: the platform beats, the sensors hear a position and resume, the store climbs again with a visible gap left behind in the series. The gap does not heal, because it happened.

The same facts, without the picture

An SVG graph is not a keyboard surface and it is not a screen-reader surface. So today’s table does not retire — it becomes the list view, fed from the same graph.ts the canvas reads, with the same controls and the same refusals. Neither view is the other’s accessible fallback; the panel test asserts one shared source rather than two lists (FR-015, SC-007).

What falls out on the Map

Once ownship state is an ordinary measurement, the track is not a feature the map has to be given — it is a query the map can already ask. The panel fetches Datastreams(‘ds-ownship-course’)/Observations through the seam and draws the locations those observations carry, in phenomenon-time order. Nothing is interpolated between them: the planner’s route is a four-dimensional curve and stays drawn as one, and the track is the places the platform actually reported (FR-032 to FR-035).

What this design refuses to draw

RefusedWhy
A node for the adaptive samplerA display of what exists must not draw what has never existed. The topic and its publish rules are real and are drawn; the absent publisher is stated in words (FR-022).
A planner → platform arrowIt does not exist, and it will not until a component that decides does. Constitution VIII. The picture answers the question instead of leaving a hole.
Edges for ctl/clock and ctl/heartbeatEvery component subscribes to both; forty edges would hide the ones that carry meaning. They are drawn as the plane, and the panel names the suppression — the only two (FR-004).
A flat line at zero for an empty seriesZero is a measurement. Nothing is not. An empty series says it is empty and a gap is drawn as a gap (FR-010).
Force-directed layoutA picture that rearranges itself between renders cannot be learned and cannot be tested. Ranks and lanes are declared (FR-002).
Position as a scalar observation resultA latitude is not a measurement result in SensorThings’ sense. Position is the location every observation already carries; the ownship datastream is what makes a series of them a track (FR-027).
HistoricalLocationsOne representation of the track, not two that can disagree. It stays refused by name, with the reason recorded beside the refusal (FR-031).
A manual “request a run” buttonIt would let the operator manufacture the loop’s cause. The demo’s claim is that the loop turns because the world diverged.
Persisting the sparkline windowsVersion 2 persists nothing between visits by design. A rolling window that outlived the page would be a second store (FR-009).

Open, and worth arguing about

  • Density. The faces above are drawn at 268 × 178. Twenty of them do not fit a laptop viewport beside the drawer, and the compact face is designed but the switch is currently a manual control. Automatic below a width threshold, as 111’s rail is? Or a control, on the grounds that a face collapsing under you while you read it is worse than a scroll bar? tasks T003
  • Where the loop’s return arc goes. It is drawn over the top, which reads well and crosses nothing — but it is a port edge, and port edges are dashed and quiet everywhere else. The most important arrow in the picture being the least emphatic one may be wrong.
  • How much of the drawer is the face again. Repeating the instrument at full size is comfortable and costs a lot of screen. The alternative — the drawer carries only what the face could not fit — is tighter and asks the reader to hold two things at once.
  • Whether the operator node should be drawn at all. It is the surface doing the drawing, and a picture that contains itself is either honest or a joke. It is drawn, with a line saying so; that decision is not settled.
Not open: whether a figure may be presented as reported when the shell computed it. It may not, in any state, at any density, in either view. Everything else here is negotiable.
