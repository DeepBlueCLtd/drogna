What the channels are for

In the earlier passes the links were lines: an arrow said A talks to B and stopped there. Here the gap between two elements is a lane with room in it, and what crosses is drawn at a size you can aim at. A reader who wants to know what SensorThings actually looks like on the wire clicks a green dot and reads one.

Requests and results share a lane in opposite directions, which is the whole shape of a query interface: the client sends a URL, the answer comes back as CoverageJSON. Both are clickable, so the tab can show a real EDR round trip without a reader ever leaving it — and the composer on the Map is one link away when they want to write one themselves.

Nothing in the drawing names a component. Six roles: what is measured, what tests it against belief, what re-forecasts, what is believed, who is told, and who asks. The three standards sit on the channels that carry them.

The question this still raises

Feature 115, FR-71: motion comes from received traffic and nothing else — “no animation may run when no message is arriving, a display that keeps moving while the broker is silent being an assertion of traffic that does not exist.” The marks above are on a timer. Under the real system they would be the observations that genuinely arrived, the gauge would be the monitor's own streak, and the EDR pair would be a request this page really made and the CoverageJSON that really came back — which is also the only way the inspector can show a payload worth reading.

The recommendation is unchanged: drive it from the running system. The inspector makes the case sharper than the animation did — an invented payload in an inspector is a lie with a monospace font on it, where a real one is the most convincing thing on the page.

Still to settle

  1. Timer or real traffic. Everything else follows from it, and the inspector raises the stakes.
  2. What a step reveals. Here a step brings in one element and the channel that reaches it, so the picture and the story arrive together.
  3. Whether the channels stay horizontal. Six lanes read cleanly left to right, but the loop is then a returning arrow along the top rather than a ring. The ring was clearer; the lanes hold more.
  4. How much payload the inspector shows. Above it is three or four fields. The full document, rendered against its master, is what the Messages tab already does well.
